As a Sales Analyst on ZURU Edge's Commercial team, my role sat at the intersection of understanding sales performance data and category strategy. Day to day, that meant pulling live sales and promotional data from retailer and market data portals, turning it into performance reads and recommendations for the brand and category teams, and flagging risks or opportunities that we were not capturing. My core focus was ZURU's Baby and Beauty portfolios, though I also picked up projects in Confectionery, on Gumi Yums, and in the newer Pet Treats range, working with growing brands like Bonkers and Mr Purrs.

01 Primary Contact for Sales & Category Data

I was the primary source of contact for providing data insights and validating numbers for retailer range reviews, category performance reviews, and similar requests across the business. This regularly meant root cause analysis, digging into SKU level, regional, or store level data to explain changes in sales patterns. I looked into promotional activity, category performance, competitive intensity, seasonality, a new product launch, stock availability, and changes happening in-store to determine what the main drivers and detractors were behind a given sales number.

Using platforms like Unify and Quantium to pull live data on a weekly basis, I learned to compile and deliver that data in the format needed based on the objective, whether that was a quick summary, a brief table, or a full breakdown. Being the go-to person for this meant staying comfortable moving quickly across categories and data sources, and being able to trust my own read of the numbers under time pressure.

02 Retail & SKU Performance Across Baby and Beauty

Across both portfolios, this came down to working at SKU level within Unify and Quantium, then translating that into what it meant for the brand's next promotional or listing decision. The type of analysis shifted depending on the vertical, and even brand to brand, from tracking a brand new launch to benchmarking years of promotional history.

Baby

Analysis in this vertical leaned heavily on promotional activity and competitive intensity. Without a strong seasonal pattern to lean on, retailer performance and customer loyalty mattered more in reading how a brand was actually doing.

03 Promo Phasing & Competitive Tracking

A large part of this was promo phasing analysis: tracking week-on-week shifts in sales patterns and comparing the current year's promotional calendar against the same period the year before, to see which timing and structure choices had actually worked better. I also worked on understanding competitor promotional strategy, mapping how their timing, structure and offers compared to ZURU's own to see where our calendar held up and where competitors were pulling ahead.

Alongside that, I compared planned promotions against forecasted values, looking at how the resulting sales and units stacked up against what had been forecast, to get a clearer read on which promotional strategies were genuinely driving results rather than just generating activity.

04 Working With AI in a Live Data Environment

ZURU's internal reporting system had AI built into it, drawing on data from Unify and Quantium. Getting real value out of it meant learning how to navigate that internal AI source properly, how to prompt and guide it so the analysis it produced actually matched what the live data showed, rather than assumptions it made on its own. That meant regularly checking its output against the source data, and over time getting better at setting it up correctly from the start so I was not correcting it after the fact. It gave me a level of comfort with AI tools that I had not built before this role.

Alongside this, I used AI to streamline the process of pulling together weekly and bi-weekly NPD, new product development, performance reports across different reporting scenarios, which taught me how to make that kind of repetitive reporting work faster and more efficient than I had managed before.

05 Case Study: Catching a Pricing Error Before It Compounded

During a major haircare promotional event in a specific retailer, unit sales on a few SKUs for MONDAY Haircare ticked up and the internal system read it as a win. Digging into it, I found the product was listed in store at around 60% below its intended price, something the system was never built to catch. The mispricing had pushed units up without a matching revenue gain, and over the following weeks it fed into a stock availability issue that dragged sales down meaningfully, a knock-on effect that would not have surfaced without going back to check in-store pricing against the numbers.

In that same promotional window, I also caught a separate discrepancy in a mini shampoo and conditioner range that had been placed on the wrong promotion. Rather than just flagging it, I used the mismatch to test what an alternative promo structure might do for the range, and the analysis pointed to an approach that could outperform the existing one, which I proposed for the next promo cycle.
