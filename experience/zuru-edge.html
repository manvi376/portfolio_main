As a Sales Analyst on ZURU Edge's Commercial team, my role sat at the intersection of understanding sales performance data and category strategy. Day to day, that meant pulling live sales and promotional data from retailer and market data portals, turning it into performance reads and recommendations for the brand and category teams, and flagging risks or opportunities that we weren't capturing. My core focus was ZURU's Baby and Beauty portfolios, though I also picked up projects in Confectionery, on Gumi Yums, and in the newer Pet Treats range, working with growing brands like Bonkers and Mr Purrs.

01 Primary Contact for Sales & Category Data

Beyond my core portfolio work, I was often the first person people across the business came to when they needed data quickly, whether that was a retailer range review, a category performance review for a specific retailer, or a root cause analysis that meant digging into SKU level, regional, or store level data to explain why sales had moved. The reasons behind a shift could come from almost anywhere, promotional activity, category performance, competitive intensity, seasonality, a new product launch, stock availability, or changes happening in store, and part of the job was working out which of those was actually driving the number in front of me.

For most of these requests, I would pull from our internal AI system or go straight to the live data in Unify and Quantium, then compile and deliver whatever was needed in the format the person actually wanted, whether that was a quick summary, a table, or a full breakdown. Being the go-to person for this meant staying comfortable moving quickly across categories and data sources, and being able to trust my own read of the numbers under time pressure.

03 Promo Phasing & Competitive Tracking

Unify and Quantium were the two portals I worked in day to day, pulling live sales data and tracking week-on-week shifts in sales patterns. I used the same data to map competitor promotional activity against ZURU's own, running promo phasing analysis to compare the current year's promotional calendar against the same period the year before and see which timing and structure choices had actually worked better.

Alongside that, I compared planned promotions against forecasted values, looking at how the resulting sales and units stacked up against what had been forecast, to get a clearer read on which promotional strategies were genuinely driving results rather than just generating activity.

04 Working With AI in a Live Data Environment

ZURU's internal reporting system had AI built into it, drawing on data from Unify and Quantium. Getting real value out of it meant learning how to navigate that internal AI source properly, how to prompt and guide it so the analysis it produced actually matched what the live data showed, rather than assumptions it made on its own. That meant regularly checking its output against the source data, and over time getting better at setting it up correctly from the start so I was not correcting it after the fact. It gave me a level of comfort with AI tools that I had not built before this role.

Alongside this, I used AI to streamline the process of pulling together weekly and bi-weekly NPD, new product development, performance reports across different reporting scenarios, which taught me how to make that kind of repetitive reporting work faster and more efficient than I had managed before.

05 Case Study: Catching a Pricing Error Before It Compounded

During a major haircare promotional event in Coles, unit sales on a few SKUs ticked up and the internal system read it as a win. Digging into it, I found the product was listed at the wrong promotional price in store, something the system was never built to catch. The mispricing had pushed units up without a matching revenue gain, and over the following weeks it fed into a stock availability issue that dragged sales down meaningfully, a knock-on effect that would not have surfaced without going back to check in-store pricing against the numbers.

In that same promotional window, I also caught a separate discrepancy in a mini shampoo and conditioner range that had been placed on the wrong promotion. Rather than just flagging it, I used the mismatch to test what an alternative promo structure might do for the range, and the analysis pointed to an approach that could outperform the existing one, which I proposed for the next promo cycle.
